# Task Plot Brief

## Task identity

- Task: Negative Priming (`T000088`)
- Construct: selective attention / inhibitory control
- Canonical implementation: Friedman and Miyake (2004) nonverbal shape-matching task

## Representative conditions

The diagram must show three rows: `No distractor`, `Control`, and `Negative priming`.
Each row is one complete prime-probe pair.

## Participant-visible sequence

1. Ready cue: blue `READY` on black; participant presses SPACE.
2. Blank: black screen, 1,100 ms.
3. Prime fixation: white `+`, 500 ms.
4. Prime response: on the left, a green irregular outline target; where applicable, a red irregular outline distractor overlaps the target. On the right, a white irregular outline reference. `F = different`, `J = same`; response window 5.0 s maximum.
5. Inter-display blank: black screen, 100 ms.
6. Probe fixation: white `+`, 500 ms.
7. Probe response: green target plus overlapping red distractor on the left and white reference on the right; `F = different`, `J = same`; response window 5.0 s maximum.

## Condition-defining identity logic

- No distractor: the prime contains only the green target and white reference; the probe contains an unrelated green target, red distractor, and white reference.
- Control: the prime and probe both contain target, distractor, and reference outlines, but all probe shapes are unrelated to prime shapes.
- Negative priming: the ignored red distractor shape in the prime becomes the green target shape in the probe. This exact identity relation must be unmistakable; use one distinctive notched irregular outline for both shapes while changing only its color and role.

## Layout and audit constraints

- Landscape, publication-ready scientific infographic on a light background.
- Three horizontal rows and seven aligned timeline cards per row.
- Compact black participant-screen cards with consistent proportions and thin arrows.
- Leave the top 17% entirely blank for post-processing title, construct subtitle, and TaskBeacon logo.
- Put phase labels above or below cards and timing labels beneath them.
- Use no photographs, people, lab equipment, brains, reward icons, or decorative scenery.
- Never put the red distractor on the right; the white reference is always on the right.
- Keep shapes large enough to distinguish but never overlapping card borders, labels, arrows, or neighboring cards.
- Do not draw a TaskBeacon logo or watermark in the generated image.
